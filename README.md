# UK Road Safety Dashboard — 2025

## Project Overview

This project analyses UK road collision data from 2025 using Power BI. The aim was to identify patterns in road collisions and present the findings through an interactive dashboard.

The dashboard explores collisions by severity, day of the week, road class, police force and month, alongside key figures for total collisions and casualties.


## Dataset & Source

The project uses the 2025 UK Road Safety Data — Collisions dataset published by the UK Department for Transport (DfT).

The dataset contains information about reported road collisions in Great Britain, including collision severity, date, road class, police force and number of casualties.

Source: UK Department for Transport — Road Safety Open Data


## What I Did

- Imported and prepared the 2025 road collision dataset in Power BI.
- Created readable labels for coded categories such as collision severity, road class and police force.
- Created KPI cards showing total collisions and total casualties.
- Built visualisations to analyse collisions by severity, day of the week, road class, police force and month.
- Created a Top 10 police forces visual based on the number of collisions.
- Organised the visuals into a dashboard to make the findings easy to understand.


## Dashboard Features

The dashboard includes:

- **Total Collisions:** 101,525 reported collisions.
- **Total Casualties:** Approximately 128,000 casualties.
- **Collisions by Severity:** Compares fatal, serious and slight collisions.
- **Collisions by Day of Week:** Shows how collisions vary across the week.
- **Collisions by Road Class:** Compares collisions across different road types.
- **Top 10 Police Forces:** Shows the 10 police forces with the highest number of reported collisions.
- **Collisions by Month:** Shows how collision numbers vary throughout the year.

- ## Key Findings

The dashboard highlights several patterns in the 2025 road collision data:

- Slight collisions account for the largest number of reported collisions.
- Collision numbers vary across the days of the week, with Friday and Saturday showing relatively high numbers.
- The number of collisions differs considerably between road classes.
- A small number of police forces account for a large proportion of reported collisions.
- Collision numbers vary throughout the year, with noticeable differences between months.
